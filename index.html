Research Overview

This project investigates the relationship between the gender composition of inventor teams and the scientific knowledge underpinning green technological innovation.

Combining large-scale patent data, scientific publication linkages, gender-related indicators and econometric analysis, the research examines how gender representation and scientific knowledge sourcing evolve over time.

The interactive research visualisations connect descriptive evidence with statistical analysis, allowing users to explore longitudinal patterns, compare indicators and interpret econometric results.

The project focuses on four questions:

  • How has green patenting activity changed over time?
  • How is gender represented in green inventor teams?
  • How does inventor gender composition relate to scientific knowledge sourcing?
  • How do these relationships vary with patent and technological characteristics?

Data Sources

The research integrates three principal data sources, each contributing a distinct part of the empirical analysis.

Patent records

PatentsView

Provides U.S. patent records, disambiguated inventor information and inventor gender attribution.

Green patents are identified using the Cooperative Patent Classification Y02/Y04S tagging scheme.

Visit PatentsView →
Patent–science links

Reliance on Science

The dataset developed by Marx and Fuegi (2020, 2022) provides information on citations from U.S. patents to scientific publications.

These links support the analysis of scientific knowledge sourcing in green technological innovation.

Scientific publications

SciSciNet

Provides scientific publication and author information used to investigate the gender composition of cited scientific knowledge.

Author gender is estimated using probabilistic, name-based attribution methods.

Visit SciSciNet →

Gender attribution and classification

Inventor gender information is based on PatentsView's disambiguated inventor data and the Worldwide Gender-Name Dictionary (WGND) 2.0, developed by WIPO.

Scientific author gender is examined using probabilistic name-based methods described by Lin et al. (2023) and Van Buskirk et al. (2023). Different probability thresholds are compared, and the results are assessed against classifications produced using the Python gender-guesser package.

Name-based gender attribution is an estimate and should not be interpreted as direct evidence of an individual's self-identified gender.

Analytical and Econometric Methods

The project combines descriptive statistics, longitudinal analysis, patent–publication linkage analysis and binary-response econometric modelling.

Selection equation

Sᵢ* = Zᵢ′γ + uᵢ

Sᵢ = 1[Sᵢ* > 0]

The selection component examines the probability of meeting the research sample's known scientific knowledge gender information criterion, as operationalised in the dataset.

Scientific knowledge outcome equation

Fᵢ* = Xᵢ′β + εᵢ

Fᵢ = 1[Fᵢ* > 0], observed when Sᵢ = 1

The outcome component examines whether a patent's associated scientific knowledge includes female-authored publications. The research considers inventor gender composition, patent year, team size, originality and technological field.

The binary selection and outcome equations are represented separately here to explain the research question. The final estimator must reflect the selection assumptions and account for dependence between the two processes.

The previously generated two-stage Probit and inverse Mills ratio estimates are exploratory outputs. A validated binary sample-selection estimator, appropriate uncertainty estimates and defensible identification assumptions are required before presenting these as final inferential results.

Python Implementation

Selected code examples demonstrate how the research data are prepared, indicators are constructed and statistical models are estimated.

1. Loading the patent dataset

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

patents = pd.read_csv(
    "genderpatent_with_gender_ratios.csv",
    dtype={"patent_id": str}
)

print(patents.shape)
print(patents.info())

2. Constructing annual indicators

annual = patents.groupby("YearOfPatentGrant").agg(
    patent_count=("patent_id", "nunique"),
    female_inventor_share=(
        "female_ratio",
        lambda x: (x > 0).mean()
    ),
    science_use_share=("Uses_Science", "mean")
).reset_index()

3. Illustrative Probit estimation

import statsmodels.api as sm

variables = [
    "male_ratio",
    "YearOfPatentGrant",
    "team_size",
    "Originality",
    "CPC_4digit"
]

X = sm.add_constant(patents[variables])
y = patents["Uses_Science"].astype(int)

model = sm.Probit(y, X, missing="drop")
results = model.fit()

print(results.summary())
print(results.get_margeff().summary())

These are selected illustrative extracts, not the complete research code. The full analysis requires the relevant data-linkage steps, variable definitions, technology controls and validated sample-selection specification.

Relevance to the Creative Economy

Although the empirical application focuses on green technological innovation rather than creative industries directly, the analytical approach has wider relevance to creative economy research.

The project demonstrates how multiple data sources can be integrated to examine knowledge production, participation, gender representation and innovation outcomes.

These methods may be transferable to research on publishing, film, music, design, architecture, software and other knowledge-intensive creative activities.

The combination of indicator construction, econometric analysis and interactive visualisation can support more accessible and evidence-based understanding of the economic and social contributions of creative activities.
